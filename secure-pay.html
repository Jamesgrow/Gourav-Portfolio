01 Problem Statement

Managing everyday payment cards shouldn't feel like navigating a marketplace.

In India, digital payment users frequently juggle multiple bank accounts and debit/credit cards. Most payment apps have evolved into crowded hubs overloaded with loan offers, scratch cards, gaming rewards, and banners. This clutter makes straightforward daily actions—like tapping to pay, checking balance, or selecting the right card—unnecessarily stressful and slow.

01

Multiple apps for different cards

Users hop between standalone banking apps and UPI tools just to check balances or pay with specific cards.

02

No quick overview of balances & spending

Account balances are either hidden behind multi-step pin flows or permanently exposed in public spaces without privacy.

03

QR payments can be difficult to find

At busy checkout counters, the QR scanner button is often buried beneath promotional carousels and investment banners.

04

Promotional clutter distracts from tasks

Flashing banners, insurance upselling, and reward games hijack visual focus during critical financial interactions.

💡

How Might We

“How might we create a clean payment experience that brings cards, spending, and payments together without unnecessary clutter?”

02 Growth / Opportunity

Why a minimalist fintech interface matters now

Rather than relying on overstated market metrics, I grounded this opportunity in direct everyday observations of how people around me interact with payment apps in India:

✓

Digital payments are becoming everyday life

From tea stalls to supermarket billing counters, quick cashless payment is now second nature for millions of users.

✓

Users manage multiple payment methods

People increasingly split expenses between salary accounts, credit cards with rewards, and secondary wallets.

✓

Financial interfaces need trust & clarity

Visual noise degrades perceived security. A calm, uncluttered UI reassures users that their funds and data are safe.

✓

Simpler, calmer everyday payment tasks

Stripping away advertising allows users to finish payment transactions in seconds without feeling overwhelmed.

🎯 Design Decisions

PRIVACY-FIRST BALANCE

Sensitive financial figures stay hidden until intentionally tapped, safeguarding users against over-the-shoulder peeking in crowded public transit or markets.

CARD RECOGNITION

Distinct card hues (AmEx Black, Visa Warm Ochre, MasterCard Steel Blue) mimic physical wallet cards for instant muscle-memory identification.

DISTRACTION-FREE UI

Completely eliminating promotional banners, scratch cards, and upselling loan tiles keeps the cognitive load near zero during critical money transfers.

PERSISTENT NAVIGATION

An ergonomically rounded floating dock anchors Home, Cards, QR, and Analytics right where thumbs can reach without awkward hand repositioning.

07 Testing + Learnings

Validation Intent & Personal Reflections

🔍 What I would validate

Since this is a concept project without live production data, these are the exact usability hypotheses I would test with real users:

Financial status readability

Can users understand their account balance and current card status in under 3 seconds?

QR payment accessibility

Can users find the QR payment action immediately without hunting through promotional tiles?

Hidden-balance affordance

Is the "Tap to reveal balance" button clearly discoverable and intuitive to toggle?

Card identification speed

Do visual card color codes successfully prevent accidental payments from the wrong account?

🌱 What I Learned

Simplicity matters when designing financial products.

Money interfaces naturally trigger anxiety. The cleaner and more predictable the layout is, the more in control the user feels.

Small privacy decisions can influence trust.

Masking sensitive balances by default was a small UI addition, but it fundamentally altered how comfortable users would feel opening the app in public.

Removing unnecessary content can improve focus.

Saying "no" to promotional banners and scratch card carousels was the most impactful UX choice in making everyday transactions fast.

Mapping the user flow before polishing UI kept the experience focused.

Iterating through wireframe stages early stopped me from jumping straight into visual styling before the core navigation made sense.
